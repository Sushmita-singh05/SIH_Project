Superposition

Learn how a qubit can exist in multiple states simultaneously and understand the role of the Hadamard gate in creating quantum superposition.

Classical Bit

Foundation

In classical computing, a bit is the most fundamental unit of information. It exists in one of exactly two states — never both at the same time.

0 Off / False
OR
1 On / True
A classical bit can only hold one value at a time. Switch a light — it's either on or off.

Qubit

Quantum

A quantum bit (qubit) is the quantum counterpart of a classical bit. Unlike a classical bit, a qubit can exist in a combination of both |0⟩ and |1⟩ states simultaneously — until it is measured.

|ψ⟩ = α |0⟩ + β |1⟩
|ψ⟩ The quantum state of the qubit (pronounced "psi")
α Probability amplitude for measuring |0⟩
β Probability amplitude for measuring |1⟩
The values of α and β are complex numbers. The constraint |α|² + |β|² = 1 ensures total probability is always 100%.

The Hadamard Gate

Visual

The Hadamard (H) gate is a fundamental quantum gate. Applying it to a qubit in state |0⟩ creates a perfect superposition — an equal mix of |0⟩ and |1⟩.

Circuit

|0⟩
H
(|0⟩ + |1⟩) / √2

Applying H to |0⟩ creates the |+⟩ state

Bloch Sphere

State |+⟩ points to the equator

Measurement Outcome

|0⟩ 50%
|1⟩ 50%

Equal probability — true randomness

AI Tutor

Tip
"Think of superposition like a spinning coin — before you stop it, it is neither heads nor tails. The moment you look, it lands on one. Measurement collapses the quantum state."
